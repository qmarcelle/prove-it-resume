Why this one earns a runtime and A does not

A dashed stroke resolving to solid, three forms closing a gap, a boundary sweeping in: these are drawing problems, and a vector runtime with real easing curves and stroke control will do them better than four CSS transitions will. Concept A had no drawing problem, only a state problem, so it costs nothing to keep in the DOM.

Ship the prototype, port the asset

This CSS version is deployable as-is, which means the decision to buy an animation is deferred until after the concept has been in front of real readers. If it is kept, the .lottie replaces the animated layers only and the settled SVG stays as the reduced-motion and no-JS state.

The risk to watch

Abstract geometry can communicate craft while communicating nothing specific. Concept A names its fields, so a reader learns what the work is; B is a mood with a structure under it. The honest test is whether someone who watches B once can say what you do. If they cannot, A is the hero and B becomes the icon system.
